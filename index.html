I. Introduction

Most of the work on this website entails concepts in RF/microwave engineering and machine learning — designing and benchmarking transmitter chains while operating RF measurement rigs and connectorized components. My day-to-day spans system modeling, test automation, and telecom subsystem analysis, with a focus on spectral efficiency, signal integrity, and repeatable lab workflows.

Current research centers on power-amplifier linearization using advanced ML, including architecture-matched modeling to align nonlinearity behavior across similar PA topologies. I received an AUS Undergraduate Research Grant for this effort and continue to refine compensation strategies that improve EVM and adjacent-channel performance under realistic drive conditions — in addition to another grant for my work on an ADRV9026-based measurement system.

More personal work includes designing a Phase-Locked Loop for a Frequency-Modulated Continuous-Wave Ground Penetrating Radar using ADIsimPLL and Analog Devices’ ADF4159, achieving exceptionally low frequency and phase error for clean chirp generation and robust time–frequency coherence (system build ongoing).

Complementing this, I earned a professional certificate in PCB fabrication (2023), built a 1.8 GHz Class-A single-transistor PA, and executed multi-layer PCB processes from layout through chemical etching. Beyond single blocks, I’ve planned complete RF line-ups in Keysight ADS to a fixed budget and target transmission characteristics (C/IMD3 and P1dB), translating datasheet constraints into practical design trade-offs.

Outside the lab, I have organized an IEEE ComSoc workshop at AUS using SDRs and GNU Radio, demonstrating analog modulation chains and digital payload construction with BPSK, QPSK, and QAM.

IV. Research: ML-Driven DPD

The main research thread was measurement-driven ML-DPD in the Advanced Radio Technologies Lab: align measured PA input/output IQ, transform the samples into memory-aware features, train behavioral PA models, and use those models to evaluate or train neural DPD systems. Most experiments used measured IQ data from the team's ADRV9026-MB evaluation-board system. Over time, the work expanded from BiLSTM behavioral modeling into spectral-loss DPD, cross-PA transfer, efficient input selection, pruning, and hybrid least-squares/neural regression.

The cascaded workflow proceeds as (1) measured PA input/output IQ → (2) train a behavioral PA model → (3) freeze it as a differentiable surrogate → (4) insert a trainable DPD before it → (5) optimize the predistorter through the surrogate with an ACLR-aware loss — so hardware-measured behavior informs training without the RF board in the optimization loop.

Contributions

  1. BiLSTM behavioral PA models that capture memory effects and support differentiable cascaded DPD training.
  2. A spectral-output-aware DPD loss that directly penalizes adjacent-channel leakage.
  3. Cross-PA transfer by freezing learned recurrent PA dynamics and retraining lightweight adaptation layers.
  4. Memory-polynomial input matrix selection methods for efficient PA modeling.
  5. Neural model pruning for RVTDNN/ARVTDNN deployment efficiency.
  6. Hybrid least-squares/neural regression layers bridging GMP-style modeling and neural feature learning.
